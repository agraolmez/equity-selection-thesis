This thesis studies whether machine-learning methods improve the measurement of conditional expected returns in the cross-section of U.S. equities and whether the resulting estimates remain economically relevant after implementation frictions. The conditioning information is a high-dimensional, point-in-time panel combining market prices and liquidity, accounting fundamentals, institutional ownership and filings, option-implied measures, aggregate market conditions, analyst expectations and revisions, and firm-related news. The underlying observations are obtained from CRSP, Compustat, OptionMetrics, LSEG Data & Analytics, the Kenneth French Data Library, and timestamped Benzinga news distributed through Alpaca. These observations are transformed into 318 source variables and 850 encoded model inputs for January 2015 through August 2025.

The predictive analysis compares XGBoost, multilayer perceptrons, Elastic Net, the provider-supplied LSEG StarMine Combined Alpha Model rank, and prespecified ensembles in a walk-forward design. Four cross-sectional targets are considered: subsequent CAPM-adjusted return, subsequent Fama-French five-factor-plus-momentum-adjusted return, long-side net payoff, and short-side net payoff. During the January 2024-August 2025 evaluation period, the selected specifications achieve mean monthly Spearman information coefficients of 0.058, 0.048, 0.067, and 0.057, respectively, with HAC t-statistics of 3.68, 7.51, 3.82, and 3.39. These results document cross-sectional predictability conditional on the stated information set, factor benchmarks, and sample; they do not constitute a general test of CAPM or the Fama-French model.

The portfolio analysis asks whether this statistical information survives the transition from ordinal scores to feasible positions. Scores are calibrated into expected-payoff units using only previously observed outcomes and are supplied to an optimizer that accounts for transaction costs, financing, stock borrowing, covariance, liquidity, factor and industry exposures, concentration, and turnover. A specification selected using February 2022-December 2023 validation data produces a 14.67% annualized net excess return, 6.32% realized volatility, a 2.320 Sharpe ratio, and a 1.34% maximum drawdown during the subsequent 20-month evaluation. Its total-return CAGR of 21.34% is approximately equal to SPY's 21.33%, but with lower realized volatility and drawdown. A separate validation-selected exposure sensitivity raises CAGR to 39.17% while retaining a 2.259 Sharpe ratio, although its leverage and market-impact requirements exceed the capacity limits of the reference specification.

The evidence is strongest for cross-sectional ranking and the long side of the portfolio. The short sleeve has a negative average standalone contribution, and the evaluation period is too short to establish persistence across market regimes. The findings therefore support conditional return and model-relative alpha predictability, together with positive net-of-cost historical performance, but require prospective evidence before they can be interpreted as persistent out-of-sample performance.
